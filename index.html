02 Research focus

Three questions I keep returning to

What is inside the model, what does it actually use, and when should we trust what it tells us about its own confidence?

I.

Mechanistic interpretability

Recovering human-meaningful concepts with sparse autoencoders, then testing which of them a downstream classifier causally relies on rather than merely encodes.

→ PATH-DISSECT, MENTIS

II.

Alignment & preference optimization

Steering a frozen model with hot-swappable helpful, honest and harmless DPO-LoRA adapters at decode time, and measuring what alignment actually changes in its internal geometry.

→ Swiss-Knife, MENTIS

III.

Uncertainty & reliability

When confidence signals mislead: grammar-constrained decoding can flip the uncertainty ranking of inputs while post-mask entropy still looks normal, so confidence monitors get no warning.

→ UncertaiNLP @ EMNLP 2026

  1. May – Aug 2026
    Paper Accepted · UncertaiNLP @ EMNLP 2026

    Constrained Decoding Can Reverse Which Inputs Look Uncertain

    M. Parvatikar · sole author · independent research

    • Built a custom decoding loop that captures logits before and after XGrammar JSON masking on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct across 100 controlled prompts.
    • Found that grammar masking reverses which inputs look uncertain, shifting ranking AUROC from 0.13–0.18 to 0.61–0.75 while post-mask entropy still looks normal, so confidence monitors receive no warning.
    • Showed the reversal depends on how surviving probability mass is distributed rather than how much is removed, establishing pre-mask entropy as the reliable signal.
    LLMsUncertaintyXGrammarEntropy
